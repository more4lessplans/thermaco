Your A/C coil: removal and reinstallation

Your central air conditioner runs on R-22 refrigerant. To rebuild the ductwork around the new furnace, the A/C coil has to come out and go back in. Your coil is reused, not replaced.

R-22 is an ozone-depleting refrigerant. It is handled only by our 313A-certified technician, recovered into a dedicated cylinder, and never vented.

Removal and pump-down

  1. 1

    Check the system before we touch it

    Gauges go on and operating pressures are recorded, so we know how your A/C is performing before and after the work.

  2. 2

    Pump the refrigerant into the outdoor unit

    The liquid line valve at the outdoor condenser is closed and the compressor runs, moving the refrigerant out of the coil and line set. The suction valve is then closed to seal the charge in the outdoor unit.

  3. 3

    Recover what's left

    Any refrigerant remaining in the coil and lines is pulled into a recovery cylinder with certified recovery equipment, so the lines can be opened safely.

  4. 4

    Disconnect and remove the coil

    The refrigerant lines and condensate drain are disconnected, and the open copper is capped to keep out moisture and debris. The coil is lifted out of the oil furnace plenum and set aside, protected, while the old ductwork comes out and the new sheet metal is built.

Reinstallation in the new plenum

  1. 5

    Set the coil in the custom plenum

    The coil is cleaned, its drain pan inspected, and it is seated on the new furnace inside the custom supply plenum. It is positioned so condensate flows to the drain, and sealed to the plenum and furnace so no air bypasses the coil.

  2. 6

    Reconnect the refrigerant lines

    Line set connections are brazed with dry nitrogen flowing through the copper to prevent internal scale. A new liquid line filter drier is installed while the system is open.

  3. 7

    Pressure test and evacuate

    The system is pressure-tested with nitrogen to confirm the connections are leak-free. A deep vacuum is then pulled and held, measured with a micron gauge, to remove air and moisture before any refrigerant returns.

  4. 8

    Restore the charge and verify cooling

    The recovered R-22 goes back into the system and the charge is verified by superheat and subcooling. We then run the A/C, check the temperature drop across the coil, and confirm both condensate drains flow freely.

Decommissioning the oil system

  • Power to the existing oil furnace is locked out and the electrical supply disconnected.
  • The oil supply line is disconnected, and the exterior oil fill and vent pipes are permanently capped and sealed to prevent accidental fuel delivery.

Excluded from this scope: physical removal and pump-out of the indoor oil tank. This is completed separately by a certified oil removal contractor.
